ChatGPT just passed 1 billion weekly active users.

That's more than TikTok. Faster than Google Maps. Faster than Instagram.

I want to be honest about what that number actually means — because I've seen two reactions to it online and neither one is quite right.

Reaction one: "Wow, everyone's using AI, I need to get on this." Panic-driven adoption. Signing up for every tool, no plan, nothing sticks.

Reaction two: "So what, it's just hype." Dismiss it. Wait and see. Same thing they said about smartphones in 2009.

Here's what I actually think.

When something reaches a billion users, it stops being a trend. It becomes infrastructure. Like email. Like a mobile number. The question stops being "should I use this for my business?" and becomes "how do I use this well?"

I'm 49. I'm a highway maintenance worker. I started figuring out AI last year with no tech background and no clue where to start.

What I've found: the bar to actually use it is lower than almost anyone thinks. You don't need a course. You don't need to understand how it works. You need to know what specific task to try it on first.

A billion users tells me the early-adopter window is closed. The question now is whether you learn to use it well or watch the businesses around you do it for the next five years while you catch up.

That's not meant to alarm. It's just where we are.

If you're a small business owner still on the fence — what's the one thing stopping you from starting?
