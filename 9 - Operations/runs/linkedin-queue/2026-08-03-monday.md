I've been quiet on here for about 7 weeks.

Not because I quit. Not because it got hard. Because I made a decision to stop performing progress and actually make some.

There's a trap in building in public that nobody really warns you about. You get consistent. You show up every day. You're posting, talking about the work — and somewhere along the way you realise you're spending more time creating content about building than actually building.

That's where I was in June.

So I stepped back. I focused on the client conversation I had in motion. I did real work on the AI content system I'm building. I had some personal things to sort. I needed to think clearly about where this is actually going.

I came back with a clearer picture. Not a finished plan. But clearer.

Here's what 7 weeks off taught me about building something on the side of a 30-year career in highway maintenance:

A reset isn't failure. Sometimes it's the most productive thing you can do when the work and the content about the work start to blur together.

I'm back. Same position — 49, still on nights, first client in progress, building this in public with no sugarcoating.

If you've been doing the same thing — building something real around the edges of a life that's already full — I'd genuinely like to hear what's working for you.
