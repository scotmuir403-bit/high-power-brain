One prompt that saves me hours every week.

I record a voice note after a shift. Two or three minutes. Whatever's in my head — an observation, something I noticed, a conversation I had.

Then I paste the transcript into Claude and type this:

"These are my rough thoughts from today. Turn them into five content ideas for small business owners who want to use AI but don't know where to start. Keep my voice. Don't over-polish."

What comes back is a week of content directions. Already in my language. Already pointed at my specific audience.

I'm not getting the AI to do my thinking.

I'm getting it to take the shape of what's already in my head and give it back in usable form.

The difference matters. Content that comes purely from AI prompts tends to sound like AI prompts. Flat. Generic. Not like anyone in particular.

Content that starts from a real voice note and gets shaped by AI — that sounds like you.

That's the thing I'd tell anyone just starting out with AI for content.

Don't start with a blank prompt. Start with a voice note. Your actual thoughts, your actual words.

Then let the AI do the scaffolding.
