The gap between people using AI well and people struggling with it isn't knowledge.

Most people who've "tried AI" know roughly what it does. They've had a play with ChatGPT. They've seen the demos.

The gap is habit.

Specifically: do you have three tasks you run through AI every single week, like clockwork, without thinking about it?

That's it. That's the whole difference.

People who are getting value from AI have boring, specific routines. Monday morning they run their inbox through it. Wednesday they use it to prep a report. Friday they use it to draft their social posts.

It's not glamorous. It doesn't make a good LinkedIn post about "transforming my productivity." It's just: here's a task I used to do manually, now I don't.

If you're not getting value from AI yet, I'd ask you this: what's the one task you do every week that eats up 30–60 minutes you'd rather spend on something else?

Start there. Not with a course. Not with a big experiment. Just that one task.

Use Claude or ChatGPT to help you do it this week. See what comes back. Adjust. Do it again next week.

After four weeks it'll be a habit. After six it'll feel strange to do it the old way.

That's how AI actually becomes useful. Not in a flash. Just gradually, task by task.

I'm building this around night shifts. If I can fit it in the gaps, so can most people.
