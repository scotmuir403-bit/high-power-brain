I got something wrong this week and I'm going to tell you what it was.

Building an AI content system for my first client. Had a clear idea of how it should work. Put real time into it.

Showed them the outline.

It was too advanced for where they actually are right now.

I built what I thought would impress. Not what they actually needed to get started.

Classic mistake. The kind you make once.

The lesson: the best system is the one a client will actually use — not the most sophisticated one. Start simple. Build from there. Their confidence in using it matters more than the system looking clever.

I've simplified the first version. We're still on schedule. The relationship is fine.

But the error cost me a few days of work I had to redo.

I could leave this out of what I share publicly. Most people do.

I don't think that's useful.

The mistakes are where the useful parts are. Anyone can share a win. The errors are where other people learn — including me. Writing this down makes it stick.

That's what building in public is supposed to be.

Not a highlight reel. A working document.
