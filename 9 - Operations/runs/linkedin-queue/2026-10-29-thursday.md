A stat I've been thinking about this week.

58% of small businesses now use generative AI. Up from 40% two years ago.

That sounds like progress. And it is.

But the same research found only 17% are actually paying for and consistently using AI tools on a regular basis.

That gap — between "I've tried it" and "it's part of how I work" — is the real story.

And I think the reason for the gap is more straightforward than people want it to be.

Trying AI feels like progress. Using it on the same task, every week, when there's no novelty to it — that's where most people drop off.

Because after the initial curiosity wears off, you're left with the unglamorous bit: building a routine around a tool that doesn't always get it right and requires you to be specific and a bit patient.

That's not a failure of the technology. That's just what a useful habit feels like before it becomes invisible.

I'm not further ahead than a lot of people in terms of experience. I'm just further ahead in terms of doing the boring part — using the same tools on the same tasks every week until it became normal.

There's nothing clever about that. You just have to go through the awkward middle bit where it doesn't quite work yet.

Most people don't. Which is an opportunity for the ones who do.
