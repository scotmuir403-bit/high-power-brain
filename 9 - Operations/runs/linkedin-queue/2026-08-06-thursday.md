First client update.

Back in May I had a call with Scott Brodie, who's building SafeSite Companion — a safety compliance app for construction sites.

He needed help turning deep knowledge about site safety into consistent content: posts, explainers, email sequences. The kind of writing that takes hours to do manually every week and often just doesn't get done.

The conversation went well. We agreed an AI content system made sense for his business. And then real life happened — schedules shifted, things came up on both sides.

That's the honest version of client work that nobody really shows.

It's not a straight line from "interested" to "signed" to "done." There are gaps. Things move slower than the LinkedIn posts about it suggest.

Where we stand now: I've rebuilt the proposal with a clearer picture of what his business actually needs — not what I assumed it needed in May.

I'm not calling this a win yet. I'm sharing it because this is what building in public actually looks like for someone who started from scratch.

No existing client base. No warm referral network. First call cold. Progress measured in conversations, not contracts.

If you're building a service business and you're at this stage — "in conversation, trying to close, not there yet" — I'd genuinely like to know how you're handling the gap between interest and commitment.
