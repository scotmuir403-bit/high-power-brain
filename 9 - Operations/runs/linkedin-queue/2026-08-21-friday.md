Something I want to put out there for anyone who's spent most of their working life doing one thing.

I'm 49. 30 years in highway maintenance and signage. Night shifts for three decades.

I spent most of that career never thinking about building something alongside it. The job was the job. That's how it worked for people like me. You showed up, you did the work, you went home.

Then a few things happened at once — debts cleared, some long overdue personal stuff settled — and for the first time in years I could actually plan forward instead of just getting through.

The first thing I asked myself was: what do I want the next ten years to look like?

Not retirement. Not escape. Just — different. Something that was mine.

That question led me to AI. Not because it was trending. Because it was the only thing that made real sense for someone building around physical work, limited hours, and a budget that didn't have much room for expensive courses or tools.

Here's what I've found after a year of building this:

You don't need a tech background to use AI tools for a business. You need patience, honesty about where you are, and the willingness to start before you feel ready.

I've got a first client conversation in progress. A content system running. And a clearer picture of what this business actually looks like than I had a year ago.

That's not a success story yet. It's a work in progress.

But if you're sitting with that question — what do I want the next ten years to look like — and you're in a physical job or a long career thinking it's too late to start something new:

It isn't. That's the honest thing I can tell you.

Happy Friday. Get some rest.
