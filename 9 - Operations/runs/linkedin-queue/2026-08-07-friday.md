82% of small businesses are now using AI tools in some form.

I shared that stat with someone last week. Their response: "That can't be right. Nobody I know is using it."

And they're not wrong — about their circle.

The gap between "82% of small businesses" and "most people I know" isn't a data problem. It's an identity problem.

The people who've started using AI don't see themselves as tech-forward or early adopters. Most of them aren't. They tried one tool for one specific task — writing an email, planning a post, summarising a document — it worked, and they kept going.

The people who haven't started yet are still waiting. Waiting to understand it better. Waiting to have time to learn it properly. Waiting for the right moment.

That wait is the trap.

You don't learn AI by reading about it. You learn it by using it badly at first, then less badly, then well enough that it changes how you work.

Here's what I'd say to anyone who's been in the waiting room:

One tool. One task you already do every week. Try it once. That's the whole starting point.

Not a course. Not a certification. Not six months of research. One tool, one task, one try.

I spent 30 years in highway maintenance before I started doing this. If I can build an AI content system around night shifts — the barrier isn't where most people think it is.
