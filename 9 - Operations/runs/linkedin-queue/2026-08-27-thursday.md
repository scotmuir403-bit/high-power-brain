205,000 workers. Job losses. AI cited as a contributing factor.

That's the headline from this month's US labour data.

I've seen a lot of takes on this number. Most of them aren't aimed at the people who actually need to hear something honest.

So here's the version I'd want to read if I were a highway maintenance worker or a tradesperson or someone in a physical job wondering what this means for them.

The roles disappearing are overwhelmingly in customer service, data processing, back-office admin, and entry-level software work. Rule-based, repetitive, computer-based tasks where the AI can simply do it faster, cheaper, and without a payroll.

If your job involves turning up somewhere, making a judgement call, using your hands, or working with people face-to-face — AI is not coming for that. Not in any realistic near-term timeframe.

But here's the bit I think matters more than the headline number.

The workers who are holding ground — who are more valuable, not less, in this environment — are the ones who've learned to use AI alongside what they already know.

Not as a replacement. As a tool.

I'm 49. I work nights in highway maintenance. I use AI to run the content side of a business I'm building on the side of that job.

The AI doesn't do the judgement. I do the judgement. The AI does the drafting.

That's the honest model.

The people at risk aren't the ones in physical jobs. They're the ones in any role who decide that understanding this isn't their problem.
