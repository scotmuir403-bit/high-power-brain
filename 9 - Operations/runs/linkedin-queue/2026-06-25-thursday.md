TIME magazine ran a headline this week: "The Small Businesses Already Replacing Workers With AI."

That headline is going to scare a lot of people.

I want to give you the honest version.

The story is real. Some businesses are using AI to do work that people used to do. I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

But here's what the headline leaves out:

Most small businesses — the ones I work with, the ones I talk to — aren't replacing people with AI.

They're replacing admin.

The two hours a day the owner spent writing social posts. The hour spent answering the same five customer questions on email. The content work that was eating into evenings and weekends.

That's not a person gone. That's a business owner getting their time back.

Anthropic published research earlier this year that said it plainly: AI is being used most to assist workers, not replace them.

The businesses using AI well right now aren't the ones cutting headcount. They're the ones finally getting out of the weeds of their own admin so they can focus on the work that actually needs them.

For anyone in everyday work — trades, services, physical jobs — the fear is understandable.

The reality is more nuanced than the headline.
