Nearly half of workers using AI at work say it's actually made their job harder.

Not easier. Harder.

New research from the automation space landed last week — 47% of employees using AI tools say they've got no idea how to get the productivity gains their employers expect. And 77% say the tools have added to their workload rather than reduced it.

That's not a technology failure.

That's a "nobody told us how to use this properly" failure.

Here's what I've found works — and I'm saying this as someone who spent the first three months using AI and getting nothing useful back from it.

The people getting real time savings aren't smarter. They're not more technical. They've done one thing everyone else hasn't: they know the specific task to point it at.

Not "use AI more." Use it for this one task, every day, until it's automatic.

For me that was first drafts. Every piece of content starts as an AI draft now. I don't write from scratch anymore — I edit. That's three to four hours a week back, every week.

For a small business owner, that could be email responses. Or social captions. Or meeting summaries.

The point isn't which task. It's picking one and going all in on it before you try to automate anything else.

If your team or your business has AI tools sitting unused — this is probably why. And it's completely fixable.

What's the one task you'd automate first if you knew how? Curious what's coming up for people.
