Most small business owners I talk to use AI the same way.

They open ChatGPT. They type a question. They get a generic answer. They edit it for an hour. They wonder what all the fuss is about.

Here's the problem: they're treating it like a search engine.

The thing that changed how I use AI — and how I build systems for clients — is a four-part prompt structure.

Role. Task. Context. Format.

**Role** — tell the AI who it is.
"You are a content writer for a small electrical contractor in Scotland."

**Task** — tell it what to do.
"Write a 200-word email newsletter for my customer list."

**Context** — give it the details.
"This month we're offering boiler checks before the summer. Tone is friendly, not salesy. Our customers are mostly homeowners in their 40s and 50s."

**Format** — tell it what you want back.
"Three short paragraphs. Plain English. No jargon."

Takes 30 extra seconds to set up.
Saves 30 minutes of editing on the other end.

The AI doesn't know your business. You have to tell it. Once you do, the output stops sounding generic — it starts sounding like you.

This is the whole game with AI prompting. Not learning complicated tricks. Just giving the tool what it needs.
