Here's something that hasn't made the headlines properly yet.

Blue-collar and trade workers are not the most at risk from AI in 2026. White-collar, office-based roles are.

The hiring data backs this up. Entry-level office positions, admin roles, junior marketing jobs — those are where AI displacement is showing up right now. Meanwhile, skilled trades are seeing growth. Infrastructure and maintenance work is short of people, not replacing them.

There's a straightforward reason. AI is software. It processes information and generates output. It can't show up on a motorway at 3am to fix a barrier in the rain.

But here's the part that matters for anyone in a physical job.

70% of roles in blue-collar industries are actually white-collar adjacent — the office-based admin, the coordination, the back-office work that surrounds the physical job. That layer is changing.

Which means people in trades and physical work who learn to use AI for the administrative side of their role or their business become significantly more valuable. Not replaced. More capable.

I'm 49. Highway maintenance for thirty years. I started learning AI because I saw this coming and I wanted to be on the right side of it.

That combination — the skill from the physical work, the AI tools to handle everything around it — that's what's rare right now.

If you're in a trade or physical job and you've been dismissing AI as something for office people — worth having another look.
