Most AI content sounds like it was written by a corporate brochure.

That's not the AI's fault. Here's why it happens and how to fix it in about a minute.

When you ask ChatGPT or Claude to "write a LinkedIn post about my business" with no context, it has nothing to work from. So it writes something that could apply to anyone in your industry.

The fix is a brief.

Before you ask it to write anything, tell it three things:

1. Who you are in plain language (not a CV — how you'd describe yourself to a new customer in conversation)
2. Who your audience is specifically (not "small businesses" — something tighter like "sole traders who handle all their own content and are exhausted by it")
3. Two or three sentences in the voice you actually use when you're talking to someone and it's going well

Then ask for the content.

The output will be completely different.

Not because the AI suddenly got smarter. Because now it has something real to work from.

Most people skip this step and then decide AI doesn't work for their business. The tools work. They just need the same context a human writer would need.

I've been building content systems for small businesses around this principle. The shift in output quality from this one change alone is the difference between content that sounds generic and content that sounds like you.

If you're using AI for content and wondering why it's not sounding right — start there.
