The AI job replacement headlines are everywhere this week.

Geoffrey Hinton — one of the people who helped build AI research — says 2026 is going to bring a wave of job losses. 30% of businesses plan to replace staff with AI this year. Customer service, admin, call centres are named specifically.

I'm not going to tell you that's not real. Some of it is.

But here's what those headlines consistently leave out:

The jobs most at risk are the ones that people already find soul-destroying. The repetitive work. The scripted customer service. The tasks that nobody considers their actual skill.

What's not being replaced?

Judgement. Relationships. The person who understands a client's actual problem — not just the scripted version of it. The tradesperson who's seen a hundred of these situations and knows what the right call is.

I'm 49. I work highway maintenance. I've spent 30 years doing physical work that requires judgement, experience, and the ability to read a situation in the dark at 2am.

AI doesn't replace that.

But here's the honest part. The people who refuse to understand AI at all are going to find the next few years harder. Not because AI will take their specific job. Because the world is changing and ignoring it isn't a strategy.

I started learning because of that. Not from fear — from practicality.

Where are you at with it?
