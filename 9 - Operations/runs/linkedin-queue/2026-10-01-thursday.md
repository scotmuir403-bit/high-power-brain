BCG published a major study on AI and jobs this week.

The headline: AI will reshape more jobs than it replaces.

Specifically, they found that 23% of roles will see AI embedded into daily tasks — not eliminated. Just changed.

Here's the part most of the coverage missed.

The people most at risk aren't the ones whose jobs get automated.
They're the ones who don't learn to use AI while the job is still theirs.

By the time their employer automates the role, they've missed the window to become more valuable than the automation.

That's the actual risk. Not replacement — obsolescence through inaction.

I'm 49. I do highway maintenance. I'm not the target demographic for AI adoption.

But I started learning it anyway. One tool. One task. One week.
A year later, I've got a client in progress and a system that's working.

You don't need to understand AI to start using it.
You need to start using it to begin to understand it.

The learning follows the doing. Always.

If you're in a role that's changing and you haven't started yet — now is still a good time.
