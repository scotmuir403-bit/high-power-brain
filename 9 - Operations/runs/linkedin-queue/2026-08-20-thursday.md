The London School of Economics published research saying AI saves the average worker 7.5 hours every week.

That's nearly a full extra working day. Every week.

I want to tell you where those hours actually come from — because the research doesn't explain it.

Most people trying to get those hours back are thinking about automation. What can I hand off completely to AI? What can run without me?

That's the wrong question at the start.

The hours come from one specific change: you stop writing first drafts.

Every piece of content I write — every email, every post, every proposal outline — now starts as an AI draft. I don't write from a blank page anymore. I review and edit what the AI produces.

That sounds small. It isn't.

Writing from blank is the hard part. Editing something that already exists is ten times faster. And for most people in business, it's also ten times less stressful.

The other place I get hours back: research prep. Before any client conversation or strategic decision, I give AI the context and ask for the key questions I should be thinking about. What used to take an hour of reading and thinking now takes ten minutes.

Two changes. First drafts. Research prep. That's where the hours are hiding.

I don't get the full 7.5 hours back — I'd be lying if I said I did. I get three to four. And three to four hours a week is twelve to sixteen hours a month back in the schedule.

What would you do with an extra half-day a month?

If you're a small business owner and content is eating your week — that's the first place I'd look.
