I'm 49. Thirty years in highway maintenance. I started learning AI tools eighteen months ago.

Here's the thing nobody tells you about starting something like this at this age.

The bit that's hard: learning new patterns when you've got thirty years of other ones. Your brain genuinely works harder. That's not an excuse — it's just biology. I notice it every time I pick up a new tool.

The bit that's underrated: everything you've built over thirty years of physical work — the judgement, the ability to spot a real problem versus a theoretical one, the understanding of what something actually costs to fix — that's not a weakness going into this. It's an advantage.

AI tools don't care how old you are. They need someone who knows what to point them at. Someone who understands what a real business problem looks like. Someone who's been around long enough to know the difference between a solution that works in theory and one that works at 3am in the rain.

That's not the Silicon Valley profile. But it's a useful profile.

I'm not trying to become a tech person. I'm using tech tools to build something around the skills I already have. That's a different thing entirely.

If you're in your 40s or 50s and you've been writing AI off as something for younger people — worth having another look.
