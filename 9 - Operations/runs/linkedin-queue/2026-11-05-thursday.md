There's a lot of noise right now about AI replacing entry-level jobs.

The WEF's latest figures. Various reports doing the rounds. The tone is somewhere between "brace yourself" and full panic.

I want to give you my honest take — not the reassuring version, just what I actually think.

**The fear isn't wrong. But it's often aimed at the wrong thing.**

What AI is replacing, right now, is tasks. Specific, repeatable, describable tasks. Not jobs wholesale — the tasks inside jobs that didn't require human judgement.

Data entry. Basic drafting. Routing enquiries. Formatting reports.

For anyone in a job built primarily around those tasks — that's a real concern. That's not spin.

But here's the thing I keep coming back to:

**The people who are going to be fine are the ones who learn to use these tools. Not fear them. Use them.**

I'm 49. I work highway maintenance. I started learning AI last year with no technical background.

I'm not saying that to inspire you. I'm saying it because if I can figure out enough to build client systems around this — you can almost certainly figure out how to use it in your job or your business.

The question isn't whether AI is changing things. It is. The question is whether you're going to be on the using-it side or the waiting-to-see side.
